## Panel Prompts

Questions to guide the panel discussion, organized by topic:

### Public Trust and Democratic Accountability

1. How should government agencies define success when deploying LLMs—efficiency, service quality, accessibility, public trust, or something else?
2. Should the standards for accuracy and reliability be higher for government AI systems than for private-sector systems? If so, how much higher?
3. What obligations do governments have to explain AI-assisted decisions that private companies may not have?
4. How can agencies maintain public trust when LLMs sometimes produce confident but incorrect answers?
5. Does government use of AI change citizens' expectations about transparency and accountability?

### Transparency and Open Government

6. What level of transparency should agencies provide about where, when, and how AI is being used?
7. Should members of the public always be informed when they are interacting with an AI system rather than a human?
8. How much information about prompts, system instructions, evaluation methods, and model limitations should be made public?
9. Can governments be sufficiently transparent about AI systems built on proprietary commercial models?
10. What does "open government" mean when some of the underlying AI technology is not open?

### Public Records and Documentation

11. Should AI-generated responses be treated as government records?
12. What records should agencies retain regarding AI interactions with the public?
13. How should public records laws apply to prompts, model outputs, and automated recommendations?
14. What documentation is necessary to allow future audits of AI-assisted government actions?

### Equity, Fairness, and Inclusion

15. How can agencies ensure that LLM-based services work equally well for different communities, languages, literacy levels, and accessibility needs?
16. What forms of bias are most concerning in public-sector AI deployments?
17. Should agencies evaluate AI systems differently when serving historically underserved populations?
18. How can governments prevent AI from amplifying existing inequities in access to public services?

### Accuracy, Risk, and Harm

19. What level of error is acceptable when AI is used to answer public questions about benefits, taxes, permits, health information, or legal rights?
20. Which government functions should never rely on generative AI?
21. How should agencies respond when an AI system provides incorrect information to the public?
22. What safeguards are necessary before allowing AI-generated content to influence eligibility determinations or enforcement actions?

### Human Oversight

23. What decisions require meaningful human review regardless of AI performance?
24. How can agencies avoid "automation bias," where staff over-trust AI recommendations?
25. What does effective human oversight look like in high-volume public service environments?
26. How should responsibility be assigned when AI-assisted processes produce harmful outcomes?

### Privacy and Data Governance

27. What unique privacy concerns arise when government agencies use public interactions to improve AI systems?
28. Should citizen interactions with government AI systems ever be used for model training?
29. How can agencies balance personalization with privacy protection?
30. What data governance standards are necessary when sensitive citizen information may be processed by LLMs?

### Procurement and Vendor Dependence

31. How can governments avoid becoming overly dependent on a small number of AI vendors?
32. What transparency and audit requirements should be included in AI procurement contracts?
33. Should governments prioritize open-source AI models, proprietary models, or a combination of both?
34. How can agencies evaluate vendor claims about safety, fairness, and performance?

### Public Participation and Co-Governance

35. How should citizens be involved in decisions about government AI deployment?
36. What mechanisms can ensure meaningful public input before major AI systems are launched?
37. Should agencies publish AI impact assessments and invite public comment?
38. How can governments create feedback loops that allow communities to identify and report AI-related harms?

### Open Data and Knowledge Access

39. How can generative AI help make government data more understandable and useful to the public?
40. What risks arise when AI systems summarize public datasets, regulations, or policy documents?
41. Could AI improve access to public information while unintentionally obscuring original sources and context?
42. How should AI-generated summaries link back to authoritative government records?

### Misinformation and Public Communication

43. How should governments distinguish between authoritative information and AI-generated interpretation?
44. What responsibilities do agencies have to prevent AI systems from generating misleading policy or regulatory guidance?
45. Could widespread government use of conversational AI make it harder for citizens to know what constitutes an official position?
46. How should agencies handle situations where AI-generated content conflicts with official regulations or statutes?

### Institutional and Long-Term Questions

47. How might AI change the relationship between citizens and government over the next decade?
48. Could AI increase transparency and participation, or might it create new barriers to accountability?
49. What governance structures are needed to oversee AI use across agencies consistently?
50. What principles should guide government AI adoption when democratic values and operational efficiency come into tension?
